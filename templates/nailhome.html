AI-Powered Early Stage Disease Diagnosis

Why Nail Health Matters

Your nails can reveal important clues about your overall health. Changes in nail color, shape, texture, or growth rate can indicate underlying health conditions ranging from nutritional deficiencies to serious systemic diseases.

Our AI system can detect 17 different nail conditions from a single image, providing instant preliminary diagnosis to help you take timely action.

Detectable Conditions

🔴 Darier's Disease

A genetic skin condition that causes greasy, warty-looking blemishes on the nails.

📏 Muehrcke's Lines

White lines that run across the nail, often indicating low protein levels or liver disease.

💇 Alopecia Areata

An autoimmune condition that can cause nail pitting, ridges, and brittleness.

📐 Beau's Lines

Deep horizontal ridges that indicate interruption in nail growth due to illness or injury.

🔵 Bluish Nail

Blue discoloration indicating low oxygen levels (cyanosis) or circulation problems.

🫧 Clubbing

Enlarged fingertips with curved nails, often associated with lung or heart disease.

🩹 Eczema

Inflammatory condition causing nail ridges, pitting, and discoloration.

🌗 Lindsay's Nails

Half-and-half nails with white proximal and brown/red distal portions, linked to kidney disease.

🥄 Koilonychia

Spoon-shaped nails that can indicate iron deficiency anemia.

⬜ Leukonychia

White spots or discoloration on nails, caused by minor injury or zinc deficiency.

🐏 Onychogryphosis

Ram's horn nail - thickened and curved nail, common in elderly patients.

📋 Onycholysis

Separation of the nail from the nail bed, caused by infection, injury, or thyroid disease.

🍄 Onychomycosis

Fungal infection causing nail discoloration, thickening, and crumbling.

🤍 Pale Nail

Pale nails may indicate anemia, heart failure, liver disease, or malnutrition.

🔴 Red Lunula

Red discoloration of the nail lunula, associated with heart failure or lupus.

📍 Splinter Hemorrhage

Thin red/brown lines under the nail, may indicate endocarditis or vasculitis.

🏳️ Terry's Nail

Mostly white nails with a dark band at the tip, associated with liver disease or diabetes.
